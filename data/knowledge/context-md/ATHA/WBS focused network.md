The Warwick Business School network is important because it is not merely a list of possible companies. It is the trust, access and credibility infrastructure that can make the first ATHA edition commercially and operationally viable.
	1	Trust is the main constraint—not identifying companies
There are thousands of companies that could theoretically participate. The difficult part is convincing a company to pay, provide a meaningful problem, involve senior people and potentially share sensitive information.
An introduction through WBS, a research centre or an established academic relationship gives ATHA immediate legitimacy. It removes much of the credibility-building normally required in cold outreach and should significantly shorten the decision cycle. 02_Research_Strategy_and_Methodology.docx (page 1)
	2	The network provides access to the right people
The objective is not simply to reach someone inside a company. ATHA needs people who can:
- Sponsor a challenge internally.
- Commit budget.
- Provide access to the business problem and relevant information.
- Arrange employee participation.
- Approve external communication and branding.
WBS academics, research-centre directors, alumni, advisory-board members and partnership teams can identify the real relationship owners and decision-makers. This is much more valuable than finding generic corporate contact details.
	3	Existing relationships produce better challenges
Companies already working with WBS are more likely to have substantive business problems connected to AI, transformation, finance, operations, sustainability or organisational change.
Because WBS may already understand the company’s context, ATHA can develop a credible challenge instead of receiving a superficial “hackathon prompt.” Strong challenges are central to the experience: they give students real decision conditions and give the company useful evidence about whether an AI-enabled initiative is commercially and operationally viable. 01_Stream_Overview.docx (page 2)
	4	It increases the probability of securing paid Challenge Partners
ATHA currently needs three paid enterprise challenges for Edition 001. This makes conversion quality more important than having a very large prospect list.
A company with an existing WBS relationship already understands the quality of Warwick students, researchers and institutional convening. The company is therefore more likely to see ATHA as a serious strategic engagement rather than another student event asking for sponsorship.
This distinction matters: ATHA needs companies prepared to fund and participate in the work, not companies that merely agree to display their logo.
	5	The partner proposition can be tailored to known motivations
Different parts of the WBS network have different reasons to participate:
- Research partners may want deeper company engagement and demonstrable research impact.
- Employers may want access to Warwick talent and recruitment visibility.
- Innovation teams may want independent examination of an AI opportunity.
- Senior executives may want structured challenge, alternative perspectives and decision evidence.
- Alumni may want to support Warwick while solving a problem inside their organisation.
- Advisory-board members may want to strengthen WBS’s position in AI-native business education.
Existing relationships make it possible to understand these motivations before approaching the company. ATHA can therefore make the right request to the right person.
	6	ATHA can strengthen WBS relationships rather than consume them
The model should create value at three levels:
- WBS or the research centre: stronger industry engagement, educational innovation and public evidence of impact.
- The company: useful decision insight, talent access and a credible AI experimentation environment.
- The relationship: a shared, visible outcome that makes further collaboration easier.
This is why WBS research centres are especially valuable. They are not simply sources of introductions; they can act as relationship multipliers. ATHA gives them a new format through which to activate and deepen their corporate partnerships. 10_USP_3Layer_and_Dependency_Map.docx (page 1)
For example, the Gillmore Centre, AI Innovation Network and Founders Hub already sit between WBS, companies, researchers and entrepreneurial talent. ATHA can convert those existing relationships into shared challenges, public moments and potentially repeat engagements. 10_USP_3Layer_and_Dependency_Map.docx (page 2)
	7	It reduces execution risk for the first edition
Edition 001 must establish that ATHA works. The first partners need to be dependable enough to provide:
- A properly scoped challenge.
- Timely access to information.
- Employees who can participate.
- Senior representation at key moments.
- Fast internal approvals.
- Payment or sponsorship commitments.
- Permission to communicate the collaboration.
Warm institutional relationships make these conditions more likely. Broad cold outreach would introduce greater uncertainty precisely when ATHA has limited time, limited funding and no previous edition to use as proof.
	8	It creates a repeatable growth mechanism
If the first WBS-connected companies receive clear value, they can:
- Return with further challenges.
- Recommend ATHA to other organisations.
- Introduce additional business units.
- Become sponsors, judges or mentors.
- Support later international editions.
- Provide evidence and testimonials for future sales.
The initial network therefore provides the foundation for an expanding partner ecosystem. The goal is not to remain permanently dependent on a small Warwick circle; it is to use trusted relationships to create the first credible proof points.
	9	It supports WBS’s own strategic positioning
ATHA can help WBS demonstrate that it is actively developing new forms of AI-native business education and industry engagement. It connects research, teaching, talent, entrepreneurship and corporate relationships through one applied format.
That gives WBS a stronger reason to mobilise its network: ATHA is not only asking WBS for introductions. It can become a mechanism through which WBS delivers additional value to its existing partners.
The strategic conclusion
For Edition 001, the WBS network should remain the primary partner-development boundary. The documents explicitly frame this as a network-mapping exercise rather than a broad market search. 11_Context_Prompt_Sheet.docx (page 1)
That does not mean focusing only on finance companies or the most visible WBS relationships. The search should expand across the full Warwick ecosystem—Gillmore, AIIN, WMG, research centres, alumni, advisory boards, the NHS network and trusted partner universities. But it should expand through existing relationships and identifiable relationship owners.
In short: the WBS network is important because it converts ATHA from an unproven external proposition into a credible extension of relationships companies already trust. That increases the chances of securing paid partners, obtaining meaningful challenges and delivering a successful first edition.

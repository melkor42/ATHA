The current working understanding is that a good SIGNAL team is not simply a group of five people—it is a deliberately constructed unit that can reach a credible, board-ready decision about whether an AI opportunity should be pursued.
The strongest setup is:
- Five participants as the ideal, with flexible teams of four to six when registration numbers require it.
- Five functions covered in every team:
	1	Domain and customer understanding
	2	AI and technical feasibility
	3	Responsible AI, governance and risk
	4	Business viability, scalability and coordination
	5	Pitch, design and decision communication
- At least one genuinely AI-capable participant per team.
- A balance of analytical, commercial, technical and communication capabilities—not five impressive individuals with overlapping skills.
Participants should register individually and provide their primary skills, secondary capabilities, proficiency and evidence of experience. Teams are then created from the actual participant pool, allocating scarce skills—especially AI capability—before filling the other functions. Degree titles alone should not determine placement.
A good formation process combines algorithmic structure with human judgement:
	1	Produce an initial team and role allocation based on capability coverage.
	2	Explain transparently why each person was placed there.
	3	Give teams a structured 20–30 minute formation session.
	4	Allow internal role adjustments while preserving all five functions.
	5	Connect teams to an enterprise challenge that fits their combined capabilities.
The practical configurations are:
Size|Configuration|Assessment|
4|A, B, C, D+E|Acceptable lean team, but one person carries both business and presentation responsibilities.|
5|A, B, C, D, E|Preferred model: clear ownership of every function.|
6|A, B, C, D, E plus a defined specialist/support role|Useful when the sixth person adds genuine capacity, but their contribution must be explicit.|
A sixth participant should not automatically become a second AI engineer. They could strengthen data integration, user research, finance, responsible AI or another area dictated by the challenge and participant pool.
Diversity should be encouraged through transparent soft preferences, without rigid quotas or excluding participants. However, age or gender data should only be used with a clear purpose, appropriate consent and an explainable policy.
Finally, a good team setup must align with the rest of the event: rotating mentors should support each functional area, and judging should reward commercial value, feasibility and responsible AI—not technical novelty alone.
The main unresolved questions are how skills will be verified, what happens during severe AI-skill shortages, whether challenge allocation occurs before or after team formation, and how the sixth role is selected. Overall, the current model is strong conceptually; its next requirement is a precise, operational matching and exception process.

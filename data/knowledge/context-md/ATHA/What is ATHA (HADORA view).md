From a HADORA perspective, ATHA is not primarily an innovation format. It is a living experiment asking whether ambitious work can become a source of human aliveness rather than depletion.
ATHA from a HADORA Perspective
ATHA is HADORA’s exploration of how innovation, business and work can happen differently in the AI-native era.
HADORA’s foundational question is:
How can we create the conditions for humans to become more fully human together?
Within ATHA, that question becomes:
Can people create exceptional enterprise solutions while becoming more alive, connected, creative and responsible—not more depleted?
ATHA is therefore a temporary innovation culture and a living laboratory for the future of work.
A new expression of the HADORA philosophy
HADORA does not exist to help people escape modern society. It explores how ancient wisdom and human understanding can be integrated with modern technology, entrepreneurship, institutions and execution.
ATHA brings that philosophy directly into:
- Business
- Artificial intelligence
- Enterprise transformation
- Education
- Innovation
- Leadership
- Organisational culture
It tests whether HADORA’s principles can support commercially consequential work—not only retreats, communities or cultural gatherings.
ATHA is where HADORA demonstrates:
Human flourishing can be an operating principle for shaping the modern world.
Designing the conditions, not the person
HADORA’s foundational design principle is:
Design the conditions, not the person.
ATHA does not attempt to instruct participants to become more creative, collaborative or responsible.
It creates conditions in which these qualities can emerge more naturally through:
- Carefully formed multidisciplinary teams
- Shared purpose and genuine responsibility
- Human and artificial intelligence working together
- Trust supported by clear structure
- High-quality spaces
- Nourishment and shared meals
- Focus and recovery
- Movement and nature
- Presence and reflection
- Creative expression
- Celebration
- Meaningful integration after the experience
These conditions are not placed around the enterprise work. They shape how the work happens.
A temporary culture, not an event
HADORA distinguishes between an event and a temporary culture:
Events are consumed. Cultures are participated in.
ATHA participants are not an audience. They become contributors and culture-makers.
They help create:
- The quality of their team
- The standard of investigation
- The relationships around them
- The atmosphere of the working environment
- The care shown toward shared spaces
- The energy of the experience
- The integrity of the final recommendations
ATHA succeeds when people stop feeling that something is being delivered to them and begin feeling responsible for what is being created together.

Stewardship rather than conventional hosting
HADORA replaces passive hosting with stewardship.
The ATHA stewardship team protects:
- Purpose
- Safety
- Boundaries
- Rhythm
- Quality
- Fairness
- Energy
- Responsibility
- The integrity of the culture
Participants retain freedom over:
- Their ideas
- Relationships
- Investigations
- Creative process
- Emerging contributions
- Final recommendations
The principle is:
Hold the container. Release the content.
When the culture is healthy, stewards remain largely invisible. When energy declines, they guide and reconnect. When safety, purpose or integrity is threatened, they intervene and lead responsibly.
Transformation through work
From the HADORA perspective, the enterprise challenge is both a real business responsibility and a medium for human transformation.
ATHA invites movement:
- From consumption to contribution
- From fragmentation to wholeness
- From professional performance to authentic participation
- From individual competition to collective intelligence
- From uncontrolled urgency to purposeful rhythm
- From technology domination to technology serving humanity
- From external validation to responsible agency
- From exhaustion to aliveness
Participants should leave with more than a presentation or credential. They should carry an embodied memory of another way of working.
Ambition without depletion
ATHA does not reject ambition. It rejects the assumption that ambition requires exhaustion, fear or unhealthy competition.
HADORA contributes the belief that the right environment can generate human energy.
ATHA therefore asks:
- Can rest improve judgement?
- Can trust improve intellectual honesty?
- Can beauty communicate dignity and care?
- Can nourishment improve sustained attention?
- Can celebration restore collective energy?
- Can multidisciplinary relationships improve innovation?
- Can structure protect freedom?
- Can AI increase human agency rather than reduce it?
Strong outputs achieved through burnout would not prove the ATHA thesis. The quality of the outcome and the health of the process must be evaluated together.
AI serving humanity
HADORA does not reject modern technology. It seeks to place technology within a wider human context.
Within ATHA, AI should:
- Expand collective intelligence
- Accelerate research
- Reveal assumptions
- Support synthesis
- Open alternative perspectives
- Reduce unnecessary work
- Preserve and organise context
AI should not replace:
- Human responsibility
- Ethical judgement
- Meaning
- Empathy
- Relationships
- Verification
- Accountability
From the HADORA view, becoming AI-native does not mean organising humans around machines. It means consciously designing a relationship in which technology serves human and ecological flourishing.
The HADORA experience arc
The ATHA journey translates HADORA’s experience philosophy into:
ARRIVE & SETTLE → OPEN & SENSE → CONNECT → CREATE → DISCERN → CELEBRATE → INTEGRATE & RETURN
The addition of DISCERN is essential to ATHA. Creation alone is not enough. Participants must decide which possibilities deserve to become real and which should not.
The experience ends with return rather than escape. Participants should carry healthier rhythms, stronger agency, meaningful relationships and different working practices into ordinary life.
The HADORA test of ATHA
From a HADORA perspective, ATHA succeeds if it creates lasting movement across eight dimensions:
- Energy: people leave with more life, not depletion.
- Integration: something changes in ordinary life afterward.
- Agency: people become more capable rather than dependent on the experience.
- Relationship: meaningful connections survive the temporary container.
- Contribution: participants become creators and culture-makers.
- Presence: ordinary life becomes richer rather than less interesting.
- Balance: work, body, relationships, creativity and meaning become more integrated.
- Humility: people leave more curious and open, not more superior or certain.
The ultimate question is:
If the physical ATHA experience disappeared tomorrow, would something valuable continue to exist in how people work, relate and create?
If the answer is yes, the temporary culture became real.
What ATHA proves for HADORA
ATHA is important to HADORA because it tests whether its philosophy can operate under conditions of real ambition, complexity and enterprise responsibility.
It asks:
Can innovation happen differently?
And more deeply:
Can the future of work be designed so that exceptional creation and human flourishing reinforce one another?
OneMundi owns and leads ATHA’s enterprise purpose and transformation methodology. Warwick Business School provides its institutional partnership, knowledge and environment. HADORA shapes the largely invisible human conditions through which the experience becomes genuinely different.
Participants do not need to be taught the HADORA philosophy.
They should simply experience its consequence:
The most ambitious work of the experience was also some of the most human.

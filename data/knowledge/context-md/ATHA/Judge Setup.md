The current working model is a five-person judging panel designed to assess whether each team has produced a credible, board-ready viability verdict—not simply an exciting AI concept.
Proposed judging setup
Judge perspective|Primary focus|
Enterprise/Domain Expert|Whether the team understands the real problem, customer need and organisational context|
Business/Strategy Expert|Commercial value, ROI, scalability and adoption|
AI/Technology Expert|Technical realism, data availability, AI workflow and implementation feasibility|
Responsible AI/Ethics/Sustainability Expert|Privacy, bias, safety, governance, social impact and sustainability|
Communication/Strategic-fit Expert|Clarity, evidence, board-readiness and alignment with SIGNAL’s purpose|
The principle is that the panel should collectively mirror the capabilities expected within each team. This creates a closed loop: participants are organised around commercial, technical, responsible-AI, domain and communication functions, and judges evaluate those same dimensions.
Scoring model
The current proposed weighting is:
- Commercial Value — 40%
- Implementation Feasibility — 30%
- Responsible AI and Sustainability — 30%
All five judges should score every dimension. Individual judges should not score only their specialist area, because that could make results depend too heavily on one person. Instead, each judge acts as the discussion lead for their speciality during deliberation.
Before the event, judges should complete a calibration session using one or two example submissions. This should establish what different score levels mean, how evidence should be treated and how disagreements will be resolved.
Proposed judging process
	1	Judges receive the challenge briefs, rubric and evidence requirements in advance.
	2	They attend a calibration session before the event.
	3	Teams submit a consistent evidence package and present their viability verdict.
	4	Every judge scores every team independently.
	5	Specialist judges lead discussion in their respective areas.
	6	The panel deliberates using the agreed scoring rules.
	7	Scores and conflicts are documented before winners are confirmed.
	8	Teams receive short, structured feedback—not only a final ranking.

Outstanding issues
The panel design is not yet fully settled:
- One document proposes five judges mirroring the five team roles.
- Another replaces the communication/design judge with a WBS/OneMundi strategic-fit representative.
- The outreach document mentions seven judging criteria without defining them.
- Other event documents refer to an eight-criterion rubric.
- The 40/30/30 scorecard contains only three top-level dimensions.
- It is unclear how communication quality, desirability and strategic fit affect the final score.
The most coherent current direction is therefore: retain the five complementary judge perspectives, treat 40/30/30 as the proposed top-level scorecard, define the underlying criteria explicitly, and validate the entire model with the confirmed judges before publishing it to participants.

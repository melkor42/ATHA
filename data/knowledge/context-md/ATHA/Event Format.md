So far, the clearest working design is a 48-hour, Warwick-based “Standard” edition for 30 participants. It is a planning model rather than a confirmed delivery plan.
Overall format
- 30 students in six teams of five.
- Three enterprise challenges, with two teams investigating each challenge.
- Friday evening to Sunday afternoon.
- Teams evaluate whether an enterprise AI opportunity should proceed across five lenses: desirability, feasibility, viability, scalability and responsibility.
- The intended outputs are a defensible viability recommendation, supporting evidence, an implementation pathway and a transparent AI audit trail—not primarily a technical prototype.
Location and spaces
Warwick is the recommended operational base in every scenario.
- Main teamwork: WBS Coventry facilities.
- Opening and final presentations: potentially the Oculus, subject to availability and suitability.
- Team areas: six identifiable working spaces with reliable Wi-Fi, power and screens.
- Additional spaces: mentor meeting rooms, private judging room, help desk, catering area, luggage storage and a quiet wellbeing room.
- Standard may use the Sports and Wellness Hub for an optional wellbeing activity and the Warwick Entrepreneurs Hub for mentoring or a short “what happens next” session.
- Accessibility, security, first aid, overnight access, cleaning and technical support still require confirmation from Warwick.
Lean and Standard remain entirely at Warwick. Ambitious keeps the working event at Warwick but may move the Sunday showcase to the Shard. That London option is conditional on funding, venue access, partner attendance and credible transport arrangements. A complete Warwick closing must remain available as the fallback.

The Ambitious timetable replaces the Warwick presentation block with travel to the Shard and a London showcase. However, its current 90-minute travel allowance is inconsistent with the estimated 2.5–3-hour door-to-door journey.
Food and refreshments
The catering plan treats food as part of participant performance and wellbeing.
Current provision would likely include:
- Friday dinner.
- Saturday breakfast, lunch and dinner.
- Sunday breakfast and lunch.
- Snacks and late-session refreshments.
- Tea, coffee, bottled water and additional soft drinks.
- Clearly labelled vegetarian, vegan, halal, gluten-free, dairy-free and allergen-sensitive options.
The current preference is a mixed-supplier model:
- Eat Away for buffets or packed lunches. An indicative quote of £12.50 per person excluding VAT, with delivery and several dietary options, is recorded.
- West One for greater hot-food variety. Its price has not yet been confirmed.
- Miss Ellie’s was considered attractive for health and sustainability but apparently lacks the capacity required.
Participants may receive a registration pack containing water, a notebook, pen, WBS merchandise and stickers. London food or a reception would only be required if the Shard finale proceeds.
Accommodation and rest
Accommodation is intended to be needs-based rather than automatically given to everyone.
- Local Warwick participants may return home.
- External and international participants may receive subsidised accommodation.
- Possible options include university halls, campus bed-and-breakfast provision or nearby hotels.
- Participants could pay the difference for a personal upgrade.
- Enterprise partners are currently assumed to arrange their own accommodation unless another agreement is made.
A quiet room and informal rest space may be provided on-site, but benches, blankets or temporary rest facilities must not be treated as overnight accommodation.
Eligibility, number of nights, booking provider, overnight security and Warwick access are still open.
Travel
No organised group transport is needed for Lean or Standard because both are Warwick-only.
If the Shard is selected:
- Rail is currently preferred for smaller groups and reliability.
- Chartered coach may suit a larger coordinated group.
- Public coach is cheaper but considerably slower and less reliable.
- Participant travel would likely be funded because the event creates the requirement.
- Partner travel funding remains unresolved.
- Accessibility, luggage, staff travel, delays and return arrangements must be included.
People and support
The Standard model currently assumes approximately:
- Eight rotating mentors.
- Five judges.
- Two hosts.
- Four operations staff.
- Two AI/tooling support staff.
- Two wellbeing facilitators.
- Two WBS/OneMundi leads.
- Enterprise partner representatives.
Essential operational controls include:
- Equal access to mentors.
- One consistent judging rubric.
- A digital participant channel such as Teams or Slack.
- A physical help desk.
- Visible emergency contacts.
- A clear escalation route from mentor or volunteer to event management.
- Technical, safeguarding and welfare coverage during all operating periods.
Budget position
The current Standard planning allowance is approximately £275 per participant, illustrated as £8,250 for 30 participants.
This is not a validated event total. Catering may apply to mentors, judges, organisers and partners as well as participants, while accommodation and travel apply only to eligible groups. Final costing must therefore be rebuilt category by category, including VAT, delivery, service charges, Warwick support and contingency.
What remains unresolved
The main outstanding decisions are:
- Final event date and scenario.
- Confirmed Warwick rooms and Oculus availability.
- Actual total attendance—the documents contain conflicting estimates.
- Enterprise challenges and partner attendance.
- Unified judging rubric.
- Final report, presentation and AI-audit requirements.
- Catering supplier and West One quotation.
- Accommodation eligibility and overnight arrangements.
- Confirmed mentors, judges and staffing.
- Funding, Warwick in-kind support and spending authority.
- Whether the Shard adds enough value to justify its cost and disruption.
In practical terms, the event currently looks most viable as a well-supported Warwick-only Standard edition, with the Shard retained as a future or genuinely funded enhancement rather than part of the operational baseline.

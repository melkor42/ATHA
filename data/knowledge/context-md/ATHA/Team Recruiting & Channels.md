The strongest recruitment approach is to recruit for the five required team functions, rather than simply advertising to “students interested in AI.” Degree subject is a useful targeting signal, but final matching should be based on demonstrated skills and interests.
Team function|Suitable subjects and programmes|Best recruitment channels|
Domain Expert|Business, Management, Consulting, Entrepreneurship, Finance, Marketing and challenge-specific areas such as healthcare, energy or supply chains|WBS programme directors, internal mailing lists, course announcements, CareersPlus and relevant domain societies|
AI Workflow Lead|Computer Science, AI, Data Science, Statistics, Mathematics, Engineering, FinTech, Business Analytics & AI, e-Business and Digital Innovation|Computer Science and Statistics departments, Warwick AI Society, Data Science Society, Engineering networks and technical programme teams|
Responsible AI & Risk Lead|Law, Philosophy, PPE, Psychology, Politics, Sociology, Global Sustainable Development, Liberal Arts and Social Sciences with Data Science|Departmental mailing lists, Law/PPE/Philosophy societies, Psychology Department, AI-safety groups and sustainability communities|
Business Viability & Coordinator|MBA, Management, Consulting, Finance, Accounting, Economics, Entrepreneurship, Innovation and Marketing Strategy|WBS programme offices, CareersPlus, Warwick Enterprise, Finance & Investment Society, startup programmes and general WBS communications|
Pitch & Design Lead|Media and Creative Industries, Creative Writing, Theatre, Film, Marketing, Liberal Arts, Design/UX and communications-related subjects|WBS Communications, Student Experience, creative and media societies, marketing programmes, university newsletters and social-media channels|
Recommended channel mix
The best channels are likely to be:
	1	Programme directors and departmental mailing lists The most direct way to target specific capability gaps.
	2	Lecturer and course announcements A recommendation from a trusted academic should convert better than a generic advertisement.
	3	Student societies Especially Warwick AI Society, Data Science Society, Finance & Investment Society, Law Society, PPE Society, Philosophy Society and relevant creative, entrepreneurship and sustainability groups.
	4	WBS internal channels Programme offices, CareersPlus, Student Experience, WBS Communications and internal newsletters.
	5	Warwick Enterprise and startup networks Particularly useful for commercially minded, organised and multidisciplinary participants.
	6	University-wide channels Warwick newsletters, careers platforms and social media can reach strong communicators and students from less obvious academic backgrounds.
	7	Participant referrals Early registrants could be encouraged to share targeted calls with relevant classmates and societies.
Recruitment priorities
The current suggested order is:
- Priority 1: WBS, Computer Science, Data Science, Law, PPE, Philosophy and Psychology.
- Priority 2: Engineering, Statistics, Mathematics, Financial Technology and Digital Innovation.
- Priority 3: Media, Creative Writing, Theatre, Film, Marketing, Liberal Arts and Global Sustainable Development.
Priority should change dynamically as registrations arrive. For example, if business students are oversupplied but AI or responsible-AI candidates are scarce, promotion should immediately shift to technical departments, law and ethics communities.
The central message should be that SIGNAL is not a coding-only hackathon. Students can contribute through business judgement, industry knowledge, ethics, behavioural insight, research, communication and design. This positioning is essential for attracting the multidisciplinary pool the team model requires.

I.5 — THE AI-NATIVE METHOD

Built the way it preaches

The team didn't just plan an AI event — it worked AI-native itself, feeding the reasoning behind decisions into a shared intelligence and keeping the human as master of the context.

Central Intelligence

Rather than feeding AI raw data, the team feeds it context and the why behind each decision — so it understands not just what was decided but the reasoning. Human intelligence defines the value AI produces.

To stress-test this, parallel intelligences were built and compared: Yanfei in Claude, Yuchen in DeepSeek.

A documentation system that lives

Living stream summaries are kept as markdown — editable and re-uploadable — with one shared-facts file as the single source of truth for cross-stream numbers.

Every fact carries a status label, so anyone can see at a glance what is solid and what is still moving. This overview is the top layer of that system.

Input
Context + the reasoning behind every decision — not just data
Central Intelligence
Holds the whole picture; human stays master of the context
Output
Summaries, this hub, and the final presentation — regenerated as it evolves
PART II

The Event

What ATHA is today: the idea, the framework that powers it, and the three pillars it stands on.

II.1 — THE IDEA

A viability instrument, not a build sprint

ATHA is a 48-hour AI-native viability event. Teams don't ask whether an enterprise AI idea can be built. They prove whether it should be — and hand back a board-ready verdict, not a demo.

The five-lens instrumentthe core assessment
01
Desirability
Does anyone actually want this?
02
Feasibility
Can it realistically be built now?
03
Viability
Does it make business sense?
04
Scalability
Can it grow beyond one use case?
05
Responsibility
Is it safe and fair to deploy?
II.2 — THE DIFFERENCE

Why it isn't a hackathon

Every design choice is benchmarked against a traditional hackathon (WHACK 2025 at Warwick) to make the departure legible. The shift is from spectacle to judgment.

A traditional hackathon
ATHA
Final pitch decks
Defended recommendations
Team rankings
Independent viability assessment
AI use largely invisible
AI audit log required
Full brief released upfront
Challenge partly concealed until the event
Sleep-deprived 24-hour sprint
48 hours with overnight rest before judging

Wellbeing is the mechanism, not a comfort. ATHA's claim to produce better judgment only holds if participants aren't burnt out. The rest rhythm, paced mentor check-ins and breaks are what make the five-lens verdict credible.

II.3 — THE THREE PILLARS

Three streams, one system

Sequenced, not parallel: Industry Partners sets the challenges and funding, Team Design turns that into team and award structure, and only then does Event Design translate headcount into logistics.

PILLAR I

Industry Partners

Imad Kasim · Yuen Lee

Sources real enterprise challenges, sponsors, judges and mentors — strictly from the Warwick / WBS / Gillmore / partner-university network. No cold outreach.

Uniqueness
  • Warwick & Monash networks, not open markets
  • Six clusters mapped by connection type
  • Ask matched to relationship, not one list
PILLAR II

Talent & Team Design

Yuchen Wu · Tuan Bui

The team is the output of a data-driven matching process. Five roles map to the five lenses; flexible sizing; soft constraints via scoring, never hard quotas.

Uniqueness
  • Not "developers in a room" — designed for viability
  • Skill matrix drawn from real university subjects
  • Every team covers all five lenses
PILLAR III

Event Design & Wellbeing

Yanfei · Jord · Radhika

Designs the whole event as one interconnected system — venue, catering, travel, wellbeing, staffing, budget — scaling together, not planned separately.

Uniqueness
  • Operations as pedagogy, not admin
  • Amazing results while protecting body & mind
  • Lean / Standard / Ambitious scenarios
